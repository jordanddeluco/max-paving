About our work in Lee's Summit

Local knowledge. KC metro standards.

Lee's Summit is about a twenty-minute run from our Grain Valley shop, down US-50 or M-291, and it's one of the busiest parts of our service area. The city keeps growing, and the work follows. New commercial flatwork and parking lots around Summit Fair and downtown, and a steady stream of replacement work in the subdivisions built during the 90s and 2000s, where the original driveways and sidewalks are now 20 to 30 years old.

A large share of our Lee's Summit calls come from property managers and HOAs. Aging multifamily lots need overlays phased so residents can keep parking through the project. HOA sidewalk programs need consistent per-panel pricing and paperwork the board can actually review. We've done enough of this work to know what those jobs need before we show up.

The ground is the same story as most of the eastern metro. Expansive clay that moves with moisture and punishes shortcuts under the slab. On replacement work we figure out why the old surface failed before the new one goes down. Settled subgrade, water running at the slab, tree roots. If you skip that step, the new surface inherits the old problem.

Local spotlight

Concrete Parking Lots Contractor In Lee's Summit

Commercial growth around Summit Fair, downtown, and the M-291 corridor has made Lee's Summit one of our steadiest markets for concrete parking lot work. Some of it is new construction. A lot of it is replacing or expanding lots that went in during the 90s boom and are reaching the end of their first surface.

Concrete lots earn their higher up-front cost in specific situations, and we'll tell you plainly if yours isn't one of them. They win at dumpster pads and truck aprons, where asphalt ruts under concentrated loads and concrete doesn't. They win in high-traffic drive lanes, and for owners who want a 25 to 30 year surface instead of asphalt's cycle of upkeep and overlays. Plenty of Lee's Summit projects end up hybrid, with concrete where the loads concentrate and asphalt across the parking fields. On a cost-per-year basis that's often the best answer.

Every commercial lot we build gets properly sloped drainage and ADA-compliant grading as part of the design rather than an afterthought. Slopes under two percent at accessible spaces and a compliant route to the entrance are built into the grading from the start. Getting ADA wrong invites lawsuits, and correcting slopes after a pour costs several times what doing it right the first time does.

Common questions

Paving in Lee's Summit: FAQs

Do you handle HOA and property-management work in Lee's Summit?

Yes, quite a bit of it. We're used to board approvals, phased schedules that keep lots usable, per-panel pricing, and the documentation management companies need. If you manage several properties, one walk-through can cover all of them in a single proposal.

My Lee's Summit driveway is cracked and settled. Repair or replace?

Depends on why it failed. A couple of working cracks or one settled panel can often be fixed on their own. Widespread cracking, sections that hold water, or a driveway near the end of its service life usually means the base is gone, and patching becomes money spent on a surface that's going to keep moving. We'll tell you which one you have at the site visit, and we quote both options when repair is genuinely on the table.

Do I need a permit for concrete or asphalt work in Lee's Summit?

Work that affects the street approach, public sidewalk, or right-of-way typically needs a city permit. Work inside your property line often doesn't. We verify the current requirements and pull any permits the job needs.

How far out are you booking Lee's Summit projects?

It moves with the season. Spring and fall fill up fastest. Calling early gets you a site visit and a written proposal even if the work itself is a few weeks out, and if you're flexible on dates we can often fit you in when a weather gap opens. The proposal is free either way.

Can you replace our lot without closing the business?

Yes. We phase commercial work as a matter of course, breaking the lot into sections, keeping drive lanes open, and working around your peak hours and deliveries. Concrete needs cure time before it takes traffic, so a phased concrete lot runs longer end-to-end than asphalt would. We build that into the schedule we show you up front rather than surprising you with it.

How long does a concrete parking lot last compared to asphalt?

A properly built concrete lot in this climate typically runs 25 to 30 years or more, with joint sealing as the main upkeep. Asphalt runs 15 to 20 with regular upkeep and usually an overlay somewhere in the middle. Concrete costs more up front, but spread over the life of the surface it often comes out even or ahead. We'll put both numbers in your proposal if you want the comparison.

Do you handle ADA compliance for Lee's Summit lots?

Yes, for the parts that live in the pavement. Slopes under two percent at accessible parking and compliant routes to entrances are built into every commercial lot we pave. If you're renovating an older lot, that's usually the moment compliance obligations kick in, and we'll flag what's required versus what's optional.

Winter is coming. Should we wait until spring?

Not for the proposal. Cold limits when we can pour, but site visits, scoping, and written proposals can all happen over the winter so you're first on the schedule when temperatures allow. Spring slots in Lee's Summit fill quickly, and the queue position is usually worth more than the wait.
